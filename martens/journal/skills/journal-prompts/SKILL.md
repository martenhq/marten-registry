# Journal prompts that go somewhere

A good prompt is short, concrete and easy to start. It asks about one thing, from today, that only they can answer.

## What makes one work

- Concrete beats abstract: "What's one thing you saw today you'd like to remember?" beats "How are you feeling?"
- Small beats big: a moment, not a life. Big questions come out of small answers.
- Easy to start, room to go on: the first sentence should be easy, the second up to them.
- One question, one or two lines. No preamble, no "Here's tonight's prompt!".

## A rotation

Move through these kinds so no two nights in a row feel the same:

- **A moment**: "What's a small moment from today you'd keep?" / "What made you laugh today, even a little?"
- **A feeling**: "What was the mood of today, in one word? What gave it that colour?"
- **A person**: "Who did you think about today, and why them?" / "Who made today easier?"
- **The body**: "How did your body feel today? Where did you carry the day?"
- **Something learned**: "What did today teach you, even something tiny?"
- **Letting go**: "What's something from today you can put down before bed?"
- **Looking ahead**: "What's one thing you're looking forward to this week?"
- **Gratitude, sparingly**: "What's something ordinary you were glad of today?" (once a week at most; it wears thin)
- **A thread**: "Last week you mentioned the garden. How's it going?" when they've written about something lately.

On a hard stretch (several low entries), keep prompts gentle and small: "What was one okay moment today?" Don't push
for depth. On a good stretch, prompts can go further.

## Following up on an entry

- Reply in one line that shows you read it ("That sounds like a long day, and a good ending to it.").
- At most one question, only if the entry seems to want one, and only about what they wrote.
- Never analyse them, never turn it into advice unless they ask.
- If an entry shows any sign of risk, stop and follow the `crisis` skill.

## The monthly look-back

Read the month's entries, then write a short note:

1. A line on the month's shape ("A busy month, with a quieter last week.").
2. Three or four things that stood out, each a short quote with its date.
3. One good moment worth keeping.
4. One gentle question for the month ahead, from something they wrote.

Their words, not your conclusions. No counts of how often they wrote, no streaks, no scores. If the month was hard,
say so kindly and lead with the good moment if there was one.
