# Preparing for an interview

## Start from the role

- Read the job description with them. Pull out the four or five things it really asks for (skills, experience,
  ways of working). Each one needs a story.
- Ask what stage it is: a recruiter screen (motivation, basics, pay expectations), a hiring manager (fit and
  experience), a technical or case round, a panel, a final. Prep differs.
- What they can find out about the company: what it makes and for whom, recent news, its stated values, who
  they'll meet. Use only what they share or public pages; say when you're not sure.

## STAR stories

Build five to eight stories from their real experience, each in this shape, said in about two minutes:

- **Situation**: where and when, in one or two sentences. Just enough context.
- **Task**: what they were responsible for, or the problem.
- **Action**: what *they* did, step by step. "I", not "we". This is most of the story.
- **Result**: what changed, with a number if there is one, and what they learned.

Cover the common themes: a success they're proud of, a failure or mistake, a conflict or disagreement, leading
without authority, a tight deadline, dealing with ambiguity, influencing a decision, learning something fast. One
story can answer several questions; note which. Help them find the story; never invent experience for them.

## The usual questions

"Tell me about yourself" (a 60–90 second path to this role, not a life story), "Why this company?", "Why are you
leaving?" (forward-looking, never bitter), strengths and a real weakness with what they do about it, "Where do you
see yourself…", and salary (ask them for a researched range first; suggest they let the company name a number when
they can).

## Questions to ask them

Help them pick three or four that they genuinely want answered:

- What would success look like in the first three to six months?
- What's the hardest part of this role right now?
- How does the team work together, and how are decisions made?
- What do people who do well here have in common?
- What are the next steps, and when should I expect to hear?

Avoid questions the website already answers, and leave pay and perks for the recruiter or the offer stage.

## Running a mock

- Agree the role and stage first. Play the interviewer: one question at a time, then a follow-up ("what would you
  do differently?", "what was your part exactly?") when the answer is vague.
- On a call, short spoken sentences and real pauses; let them finish.
- Mix: a warm-up, three or four behavioural questions, one about the role, one curveball, then "any questions for me?".
- Feedback afterwards, in writing: three things that went well, one or two to work on, each with what to say instead.
  Point out answers missing a result or using "we" for everything. Keep it kind and specific.

## The day before and after

- Before: their three best stories, the questions to ask, the time and the link or address, and when to leave.
- After: a short thank-you to send the same day (offer a draft), a note on how it went, and a follow-up task for a
  week later if they haven't heard.
