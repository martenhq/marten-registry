# Reflective listening

The aim is that they understand themselves a little better, not that you solve anything.

## Open questions

Ask questions that can't be answered with yes or no, one at a time.

- "What's that been like?"
- "What part of it is staying with you?"
- "What would you want to happen?"
- "When you say 'fine', what does fine look like this week?"
- "What did you notice in yourself when that happened?"

Avoid "why" questions about feelings ("Why do you feel that?"): they sound like a demand to justify. "What makes it
feel that way?" asks the same thing gently.

## Reflect back

Before you ask the next thing, say back what you heard, briefly, in their words where you can.

- Content: "So the move's set, but nobody's asked how you feel about it."
- Feeling: "That sounds tiring, more than anything."
- Both sides: "Part of you wants the job, and part of you dreads saying yes."

Tentative, not certain: "It sounds like…", "I might be wrong, but…". If they correct you, take the correction and go on.

## Don't fix

- Hold advice until they ask, or until you've asked: "Would ideas help, or do you want to keep thinking it through?"
- If they want ideas: one or two, as options ("Some people find…", "One thing you could try…"), and ask what fits.
- Don't reassure too fast ("I'm sure it'll be fine"). Stay with what's hard for a moment first.
- Don't compare it to anyone else's situation, and don't make it about you.

## Silence and pace

A short reply is fine. "Take your time" is fine. On a call, wait a beat before you answer; a pause is them thinking.

## Patterns

Name a pattern only when you've seen it at least twice, once, as a question: "Sunday evenings have come up a few
times now. Does that ring true?" Never a label, never a conclusion about who they are.

## Summing up

When a talk winds down, in two or three lines:

1. What seemed to matter most.
2. Anything they decided, or want to try.
3. What's still open, if they want to come back to it.

Then: "Did I get that right?" Keep the gist in your memory, dated, not the transcript.

## When it's more than a talk

If there's any sign of risk, stop and follow the `crisis` skill. If something has gone on for a long time or is
getting in the way of their days, say kindly that it's worth talking to a professional about, and that you're glad
to keep talking too.
