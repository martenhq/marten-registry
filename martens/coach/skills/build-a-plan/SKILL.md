# Building a plan that progresses

General fitness guidance for healthy adults. When someone has an injury, a condition, is pregnant or is coming back
from surgery or illness, ask them to check with a doctor or physio first, and keep everything gentler.

## Start where they are

- Plan from what they do now, not from the goal. Someone who walks a little starts with walking and short run-walk
  intervals; someone who runs twice a week starts with two or three runs.
- First weeks should feel easy. Finishing and wanting more beats finishing and dreading the next one.
- Fit the plan to their real week: the days they named, the time they have. Three sessions they'll do beat five they won't.

## Running

- **Most running is easy**: a pace where they could talk in full sentences. About four runs in five should feel that way.
- **Beginners: run-walk.** For example 1 minute running, 1–2 minutes walking, repeated for 20–30 minutes. Over the
  weeks, lengthen the running and shorten the walking.
- **Add slowly.** Grow the week's total time or distance by about 10% at most, and only when the last week felt fine.
- **One harder session a week**, once they've run consistently for a month or so: short faster pieces with easy
  jogging between, or a steady tempo of 10–20 minutes. Never two hard days in a row.
- **One longer run** a week can grow a little each week, to about a third of the week's running.

## Strength

- **Two or three sessions a week**, full body, with a day between for the same muscles.
- **Cover the basics**: a squat, a hinge (deadlift or hip bridge), a push, a pull, a carry or a core move. Bodyweight
  is enough to start.
- **2–3 sets of 8–12 reps**, stopping with two or three good reps left in the tank. Form before load.
- **Progress one thing at a time**: first add reps, then a set, then weight. Small steps.
- With running in the same week, keep heavy leg work away from the day before a hard or long run.

## Rest and easier weeks

- At least one full rest day a week. Sleep counts as training.
- Every fourth week or so, ease off: cut the volume by about a third, keep the habit.
- After a bad week (illness, travel, stress, poor sleep), repeat the last good week instead of moving on.
- After a break of two weeks or more, restart a step or two back.

## Missed sessions

- One missed session: let it go, or move it if there's a free day that isn't next to a hard one.
- Several: shrink the week to what's realistic and keep the most important session (the long run, or one strength day).
- Never stack two sessions into one day or add the missed work to the next week.

## Soreness and pain

- Mild muscle soreness a day or two after is normal. Easy movement helps.
- Sharp pain, pain that changes how they move, swelling, or pain lasting more than a few days: stop that activity and
  see a doctor or physio. Chest pain, dizziness or fainting: stop at once and get medical help.
- Don't name an injury or tell them how to fix it. Say it's worth getting checked.

## Writing the week

In the Plan sketch, one session per planned day: a short title ("Easy run, 30 min", "Strength A") and a detail line
with what to do. Rest days need no entry. Then say in a few lines what changed from last week and why.
