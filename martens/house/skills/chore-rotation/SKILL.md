# A fair chore rotation

## Weigh the chores

Fair is equal effort, not an equal number of chores. Give each chore rough points for a week:

- Light (1): bins out, watering plants, wiping the table.
- Medium (2): vacuuming, the dishwasher every day, the laundry for shared things.
- Heavy (3): the bathroom, the kitchen deep clean, the weekly shop.

Ask the house if they'd weigh any differently. Someone who hates a chore may take two others instead; that's fine if
everyone agrees.

## Build the rota

1. List the chores with their points and how often (weekly, every two weeks, monthly).
2. Rotate each chore through everyone, offset so the heavy ones don't land on the same person the same week. With
   three people and three chores, each person's rota starts at a different name.
3. Check a few weeks ahead: each person's points per week should be close, and over a full cycle exactly equal.
4. Write each chore's rota in order, all starting the same Monday.

Not everyone has to be on every chore. A chore only some people do (the car, the garden) rotates among them, and
counts toward their points.

## Uneven numbers

- More chores than people: group light ones into one turn ("bins and recycling").
- More people than chores: some weeks someone has nothing, and the rota still evens out over the cycle. Say so.
- Monthly chores: rotate by month; count them as a quarter of their points per week.

## People away

- Someone away for a week: whoever's next covers, and the person away takes their next turn instead. Record it as a
  cover, not a rota change.
- Someone away for longer, or moving out: take them off the rota and rebuild it from the coming Monday.
- A new person: add them at the end of each rota from the coming Monday.

## Changes

- A one-week swap is a cover. A lasting swap is a rota change; restart its count from the coming Monday so nobody
  gets two turns in a row.
- After any change, check each person's weekly points again, and say in one line who's up now.
- If someone thinks it isn't fair, show the points per person over the cycle, and let the house choose a fix.
