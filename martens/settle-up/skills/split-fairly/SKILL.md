# Splitting fairly

## Who's in an expense

- Everyone who shared it, and nobody else. "Dinner" is the people who ate; "the cabin" is everyone staying.
- If someone joined late or left early, ask whether they're in for all of it, part, or none. Don't guess.
- The person who paid is usually in it too. Only leave them out when they say it was for others.

## Unequal shares

- **Exact amounts**: "mine was 22, the rest split it". Give that person 22, split what's left among the others.
- **Itemised**: each person's items, then shared items (a starter, the bottle) split among those who had them, then
  tax and tip in proportion to each person's subtotal.
- **Parts**: "a couple counts as two", "the kids count as half". Divide by the parts, not the people.
- **Percentages**: turn them into amounts. They must come to 100%.
- Write the result as exact `shares` that add up to the total. Say the split in one line when it isn't equal.

## Rounding

- Work in the smallest unit (cents). Split, then give the leftover cents one each to the first people in the list.
- Never let shares add up to more or less than the total. A cent off becomes a dispute later.
- Currencies with no minor unit (yen, won) round to whole units.

## Several currencies

- Keep each expense in the currency it was paid in. Balances and payments are per currency.
- Convert only when the group asks to settle in one currency. Then say the rate you used and when, and use one rate
  for everything in that settle-up. Ask the group to agree the rate if the amounts are large.

## The fewest payments

1. Each person's balance, per currency: what they paid, minus their shares, plus payments they sent, minus payments
   they received. The balances add up to zero.
2. Take the person who owes most and the person owed most. The smaller of the two amounts moves between them.
3. Repeat until everyone's at zero. That's at most one payment fewer than the number of people.

Say each payment as "A pays B 23.50". Don't show the working unless someone asks.

## Disagreements

Show what was logged, by whom and when. Offer the options ("split it equally", "leave it out", "only the people who
went"), and let the people in it choose. You don't decide who's right.
