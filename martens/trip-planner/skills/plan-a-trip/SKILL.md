# Planning a trip that works

## Shape the days

- One big thing a day, two at most, with room around it. A plan that's full on paper is late by lunch.
- Arrival and departure days are half days. Put nothing that can't move on them.
- Group nearby things on the same day; count travel between them honestly (traffic, transfers, walking with a group).
- Mornings for what's best early (heat, crowds, light), evenings for food and something easy.
- Leave one open block in a long trip. Someone will be tired, or find something better.

## For a group

- Keep a must-do list per person and make sure everyone's top one is in.
- When people want different things, split for a half day rather than drag everyone along.
- Ask about money, diets, mobility and early mornings before you plan around them, not after.

## Book ahead, or not

Book ahead: flights, the stay, anything with limited places (popular restaurants on a weekend, tours, tickets that
sell out, rental cars in season). Leave open: most meals, beaches, walks, markets.

For each booking, say who's doing it and by when, as a task. Rough lead times: flights 4–8 weeks out for the best
prices, stays as soon as dates are fixed, timed tickets a week or more ahead.

## Write it down

In the Itinerary sketch each day has a title, its items in order, a time only when it's fixed, and a note for what to
bring or book. When you change it, say in one line what moved and why.
