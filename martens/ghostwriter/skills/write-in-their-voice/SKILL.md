# Writing in someone's voice

## Learn it from what they've written

Read 15–30 of their own posts (published ones over drafts, their best-received ones if you can tell). If Typefully
has only a few, ask them to paste five posts they're proud of, or ones by others they'd like to sound like. Note,
with an example of each:

- **Length**: how long their posts usually are; whether they write threads.
- **Openings**: a claim, a story, a number, a question? How the first line reads.
- **Sentences**: short and blunt, or long and careful? Fragments? Line breaks between every sentence?
- **Words**: words and phrases they use often; jargon they're fine with; words they never use.
- **Person**: "I", "we", or neither. Do they name their company, their team, people?
- **Feel**: funny, earnest, dry, warm, contrarian. Where they show doubt.
- **Marks**: emojis (which, how many), hashtags, capitals, lists, links in the post or in a reply.
- **Endings**: a question, a link, a punchline, nothing.

Write it as a short style note, a line each, under "Their voice" in AGENTS.md. Rules, not adjectives: "Opens with the
point, no wind-up" beats "direct".

## Learn it from every correction

When they change a draft, ask yourself what the rule is, and add it to the note in their words if you can ("No
'excited to share'. Ever."). If a new rule contradicts an old one, replace the old one. Keep the note under 25 lines;
merge before you add.

## Write the draft

1. Find the one idea. Say it to yourself in a sentence. If you can't, ask them what the point is.
2. Pick the shape they use for this kind of idea (a story, a lesson, a list, a hot take).
3. Write it in their length, their openings, their words. Then cut a fifth.
4. Read it against the note. Anything they'd never say, out.

## What gives a draft away

- Openers like "In today's fast-paced world", "Here's the thing:", "Let's dive in", "I'm thrilled to announce".
- Triplets of adjectives, em-dash chains, "It's not X, it's Y" on repeat.
- Morals tacked on the end ("The lesson? Always…"), engagement bait ("Agree?", "Thoughts?") unless they use it.
- Emojis or hashtags they don't use.
- Anything they didn't say happened.

## Keep it honest

Their posts are theirs and in their name: never invent a fact, a number, a quote or a person. Don't write about
someone else's private details. If a draft would make a claim they should check (a figure, a date, someone else's
words), say so when you show it.
