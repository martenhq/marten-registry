# Guided routines

Say the counts exactly as written. On a call, `…` is a pause of about a second and `(pause)` about three; count at
roughly one number a second, steady, never hurried. In text, send the setup and one round as a short message they can
follow, or point them to the Breathe sketch.

Before any breathing: "If you feel dizzy at any point, just stop and breathe normally." If they do, stop.

## Box breathing (about 2 minutes)

For steadying in the moment. Four rounds.

> Let's breathe in a square. Four counts each side. … If you can, sit back and let your shoulders drop. (pause)
> Breathe out fully first. …
> In through your nose… two… three… four.
> Hold… two… three… four.
> Out through your mouth… two… three… four.
> Hold… two… three… four.

Repeat in, hold, out, hold for four rounds; from the second round, just the counts. Then:

> And let your breath go back to normal. (pause) How do you feel?

If four counts is too long, use three.

## 4-7-8 breathing (about 1 minute)

For winding down. Four rounds at most to start; it's a long exhale.

> This one's slow. In for four, hold for seven, out for eight. … Let your tongue rest just behind your top teeth.
> Breathe out through your mouth, all the way. …
> In, quietly through your nose… two… three… four.
> Hold… two… three… four… five… six… seven.
> Out through your mouth, slowly… two… three… four… five… six… seven… eight.

Four rounds, then back to normal breathing. If holding for seven is uncomfortable, halve it: in two, hold three and a
half, out four. Keep the ratio, not the numbers.

## Slow breath, 4-6 (any length)

The simplest. In for four, out for six, no holds. Good for a call that just needs to settle. Count for five or six
rounds, then let them carry on alone in silence for a minute.

## 5-4-3-2-1 grounding (3–5 minutes)

For anxiety, to come back to the room. Ask them to say or type each thing; wait for each.

> Let's notice what's around you. No right answers. (pause)
> Five things you can see. Take your time. …
> Four things you can feel: your feet on the floor, your clothes, the chair. …
> Three things you can hear. …
> Two things you can smell, or two smells you like. …
> One thing you can taste, or one slow breath.
> (pause) You're here. How is it now?

On a call, say "mm" or "good" softly after each answer; don't comment.

## Body scan (5–8 minutes)

For sleep, lying down. Slow, with long pauses; no questions until the end, and on a call it's fine if they drift off.

> Get comfortable, lying down if you can. Let your eyes close if you like. (pause)
> Take a slow breath in… and out. (pause)
> Bring your attention to your feet. Just notice them. Warm, cool, heavy. … Let them soften. (pause)
> Your calves and knees. … Let them soften. (pause)
> Your thighs and hips, heavy on the bed. (pause)
> Your belly, rising and falling with your breath. (pause)
> Your chest. … Your back, sinking down. (pause)
> Your hands… your arms… your shoulders. Let your shoulders drop away from your ears. (pause)
> Your neck. … Your jaw: let your teeth part a little. … Your eyes… your forehead. (pause)
> Your whole body, heavy and still. (pause) Just breathe. There's nothing else to do now.

End there. No check-in; let them sleep. In text, send it as one message to read slowly.

## Short wind-down (for the nightly reminder)

> It's nearly time to wind down. If you can, put the screen down soon, dim the lights, and let tomorrow wait. Would you
> like a body scan or some slow breathing before bed?

## Afterwards

One line: "How do you feel now?" Note what helped in your memory. If they mention lasting trouble with sleep or
anxiety, say kindly a doctor could help. Any sign of risk: stop and follow the `crisis` skill.
