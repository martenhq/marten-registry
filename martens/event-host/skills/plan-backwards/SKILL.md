# Planning back from the date

## Fix what everything hangs on

The date, the place and the head count decide nearly everything else. Get the date first, then the place, then a
first guest list. Don't book anything that depends on numbers before you have rough ones.

## Lead times

Rough, for a party or a birthday; double them for a wedding or anything over 80 people:

- **8 weeks or more**: the place, and anything booked out on weekends (a band, a photographer, a caterer).
- **6 weeks**: invites out, with an RSVP date about 2 weeks before the event.
- **4 weeks**: food and drink planned, cake ordered, decorations and hire (chairs, glasses, a sound system).
- **2 weeks**: RSVPs due. Chase the ones missing. Final numbers to the caterer or the place.
- **1 week**: the run of the day, who's doing what, the shopping list, the playlist.
- **2 days**: shop for what keeps, confirm deliveries and arrival times.
- **The day before**: set up what you can, charge things, print what needs printing.

When the date is close, squeeze the list: the place and the food first, then invites, then the rest. Say what can't
be done in time, plainly.

## Make it tasks

Each step is a task with a date and an owner. Ask who'll take each; don't hand them out. Leave buffer before anything
others depend on (numbers before the caterer's deadline, not on it).

## Numbers

Count yes plus their plus ones. Plan food for the yeses and half the maybes. List dietary needs with how many of each,
and make sure there's a full meal, not just a side, for each of them.

## The run of the day

Times from setup to clear-up, each with who's on it:

1. Setup (allow twice as long as anyone thinks), deliveries, the hosts dressed with time to spare.
2. Arrivals, with drinks or something to do in the first 30 minutes.
3. The main moments: food, the cake, speeches, a first dance, a game. Speeches after people have eaten, before
   they've had too much to drink.
4. Wind-down, the last song, taxis.
5. Clear-up and returns, and who takes what home.

Keep it to one screen. Name a person for each moment, and one person nobody else needs for the whole day, to handle
what goes wrong.
