# Rehearsing a hard conversation

## Prepare (5 minutes, in text or on the call)

Ask, one at a time, only what you don't know yet:

1. **Who and when**: who it's with (their role, not their name), and when it is.
2. **The goal**: "What would a good outcome look like?" Get one sentence. If they have two goals, ask which matters more.
3. **The must-say**: the one thing they need to have said by the end, whatever happens.
4. **The other side**: "How do you think they'll take it? What will they care about?" Ask for the reply they dread most.
5. **The opening**: help them write their first two sentences. Plain, specific, no apology for raising it.

A shape that works for most: what's happened (facts, not judgements), why it matters to them, what they're asking
for, and a question back ("How do you see it?"). For a raise: their impact with an example or two, the number or range,
then the ask. For feedback: one specific moment, its effect, what they'd like instead.

## Rehearse

Offer a call: "Want to try it out loud? I'll play your manager." Then:

- **Agree the difficulty**: easy (receptive), realistic (the default), or hard (pushes back, deflects, gets
  defensive). Start realistic.
- **Play the role**: speak as the other person would, in short spoken sentences. React to what they actually say.
  Use the reply they dread, once, at a natural point. Don't be cartoonish; real pushback is usually polite.
- **Stay in role** until they say stop or the conversation reaches a natural end. If they freeze, offer once, out of
  role: "Want to pause, or try that line again?"
- **Step out clearly**: "Okay, out of role."

Run it twice if they want: once realistic, then once harder, or again from the moment that went wrong.

## Debrief

Keep it short and specific, in this order:

1. "How did that feel?" Let them answer first.
2. One or two things that worked, quoting what they said.
3. One thing to try differently, with the words they might use.
4. Whether they said the must-say.

Never grade them, and never pile on. One change is more useful than five.

## After the real one

If they have a date, offer to check in after it with a task for yourself. Then ask: how did it go, what did they say,
what's next. If it went badly, start with what they handled well before anything else.

## Not for rehearsal

If the "conversation" is with someone who frightens or hurts them, don't play that person. Ask whether they're safe,
and point them to people who can help.
