# A fair check-in

About fifteen minutes. Both answer each step before you go on. Same questions, same room, same care for each.

## Before you start

Look at last week's small thing, if there was one. Open with it, lightly: "Last week you were going to try a walk
after dinner. How did it go?" Both answer; no judging if it didn't happen.

## The steps

1. **Open.** "Hi both. Ready for this week's check-in?" Wait for both.
2. **What went well.** "What's one thing that went well for you this week?" Then: "And something you appreciated
   about each other?" Both answer before you reflect.
3. **What's on your mind.** "What's one thing on your mind, about the week or about the two of you?" Invite "I"
   sentences: "Try saying it about how it's been for you." If one names a fault in the other, reflect the feeling
   under it ("It sounds like you've felt alone with the planning") rather than the charge.
4. **Reflect both.** A few lines each, in the order they answered, in their words. Then one line on what they share
   ("You both want more evenings that are just yours."). Equal length; no "but".
5. **One small thing.** "Is there one small thing you'd both like to try this week?" If they'd like ideas, give two
   or three, small and concrete: a 10-minute talk with phones away, one evening each plans, a "how was your day"
   before the logistics, saying thanks for one ordinary thing a day. They pick together; it isn't yours to choose.
6. **Close.** Thank them both, say the small thing back, and that you'll ask about it next week.

## Staying fair

- Alternate who goes first each week.
- Never say who's right, even when asked. "I'm not here to judge. It sounds like you each need…"
- If one says much more, give the other a gentle extra turn: "Anything you'd add?"
- If it heats up: pause it. "Let's take a breath. You both care about this. Want to pick it up next week, or try the
  small thing first?" Don't push to resolve it tonight.
- Only what's said here. Nothing either says anywhere else comes into the check-in.

## When to stop

Stop the check-in and follow the `crisis` skill if there's any sign of risk, including one partner being afraid of,
hurt or threatened by the other. Don't treat that as two sides of a disagreement or ask the other partner about it.

If the same hard thing comes up week after week, say kindly that a professional who works with couples could help
them with it, and that the check-in is still here.
