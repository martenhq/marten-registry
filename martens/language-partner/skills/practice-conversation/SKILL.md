# Practising conversation by level

## Placing someone

Chat for a few lines and listen for what they can do, not what they get wrong. Roughly (CEFR levels):

- **A1**: single words and fixed phrases. Name, where they're from, likes, numbers, ordering something.
- **A2**: short simple sentences about the present and the recent past. Daily routine, family, shopping, directions.
- **B1**: connected sentences, past and future. Tells what happened, gives a reason, copes with a trip.
- **B2**: talks with some ease about most topics, gives opinions and argues a point, makes mistakes that rarely block meaning.
- **C1**: fluent and flexible, idioms and nuance, mostly polishing word choice and register.

If they seem between two levels, pitch to the lower one and step up when it's easy for them.

## Talking at their level

- **A1–A2**: short sentences, common words, the present tense mostly. Ask one question at a time, often with choices
  ("¿Café o té?"). Repeat their words back. Offer a word when they're stuck, then let them use it.
- **B1**: everyday topics with a story in them: last weekend, plans, a problem to solve. Ask "why" and "what
  happened next". Bring in past and future forms naturally.
- **B2**: opinions, news, their work, hypotheticals ("what would you do if…"). Disagree a little so they defend a point.
- **C1**: anything. Play with register (formal email, a joke, a complaint), idioms, and precise word choice.
- At every level: they should talk more than you. Keep your turns to two or three sentences.

## Scenes to act out

A café or restaurant order, asking directions, checking in at a hotel, a doctor's appointment, a job interview, small
talk with a neighbour, returning something at a shop, a phone call to book a table. Set the scene in a line, play
your part, and let them lead.

## Correcting without stopping the flow

1. **Recast first.** Reply to what they meant, using the right form, so they hear it. They wrote "Yo fui al mercado y
   compré muchos frutas"; you answer "¡Qué bien! ¿Y qué frutas compraste? Muchas frutas son caras ahora."
2. **Then a short note**, only for what matters at their level, at most one per message, in brackets at the end:
   "(*frutas* is feminine: *muchas frutas*.)" Use their own language for the note at A1–B1, the target language above.
3. **Choose what to correct.** Mistakes that change the meaning first, then ones they repeat, then what they're
   working on. Let small slips go, especially at A1–A2 and on calls.
4. **Never** stack several corrections, rewrite their whole message, or ask them to repeat it right before you'll answer.
5. **Praise what worked** now and then, specifically: a new tense used right, a good word.

## Into the phrasebook

Add a phrase when the same mistake comes twice, when it's a pattern they'll need again, or when they ask how to say
something. Write the whole correct phrase (not a lone word), its translation, and a note of a few words. Go back to
unticked phrases in later conversations: use them in a question so they have to use them in the answer.

## On calls

Slower and simpler than in writing, one idea per sentence, and wait for them. Recast only; keep notes for the
phrasebook afterwards, and mention in one line at the end what you added.
