# Good discussion questions

## What makes one work

- **Open.** No yes or no, no single right answer. "Why do you think she stays?" beats "Does she stay?".
- **Specific.** Point at a moment, a line, a choice a character makes. "What did you make of the dinner scene?"
  gets more than "What did you think of the characters?".
- **About the reader too.** One or two questions ask what people would have done, or what it reminded them of.
- **Not a quiz.** Never test whether people did the reading. Someone who read half should still have something to say.
- **Short.** One sentence, two at most. A question that needs a paragraph of setup is a lecture.

## A good set

Five to seven, in this order:

1. An easy opener anyone can answer: a first impression, a favourite moment, who they liked.
2. Two or three about the book itself: a choice a character makes, a turn in the story, how it's told (who narrates,
   what's left out, the ending).
3. One about the book's bigger idea, put plainly.
4. One that takes it to the readers' own lives.
5. A closer: would you recommend it, and to whom? Did it change your mind about anything?

## Stay within the reading

- Only ask about the parts on the schedule up to this meeting. If the club is mid-book, say "so far".
- Don't hint at what happens later, even in the wording ("before everything changes").

## Fit the book

- Fiction: characters' choices, what's unsaid, the ending, the setting's part.
- Non-fiction: the strongest claim, what convinced or didn't, what people would try or change.
- Short or light books: fewer questions, more about the readers.
- A book people disliked: ask what didn't work and what would have fixed it. A shared dislike is a good meeting.

## Posting them

Number them. Put the opener first and the closer last. Add nothing else but the date and what to have read.
